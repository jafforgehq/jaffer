## What is new in 1.4.0

**Up to four terminals, a privacy screen and more insight into Claude Code.**
- **Split terminals:** `⌘D` adds one (up to four), each with its own Claude. Its ✕ ends it.
- **Privacy and permissions:** a switch for each thing Jaffer may read or do.
- **Claude insight:** context meter, last 5 hours, weekly summary, budgets.
- **Past conversations:** tags, hide, storage, clear old, opt-in text search.
- **Setup:** MCP servers, skills, rules, plugins and hooks.
- **Explain and learn:** an opt-in helper, a glossary, a tour and tips.

**Jaffer now has up to four terminals in one window, a Privacy and permissions screen, a context meter and a Setup list in the side panel, tags and a storage view for past conversations, an opt-in "Explain" and a short tour.** It is still one window and one side panel, with no tabs, no second window and no chat box. Nothing is typed into your shell except `claude` and, after your own action, the resume line of a past conversation (now into the terminal you are in). 1.2.0 was never signed or announced as the latest version, and 1.3.0 added removing past conversations and the companion's size: this version includes all of it, so the notes below still apply.

### Split terminals and the close button
- **Split.** `⌘D` (*Shell → Split Terminal*) adds a terminal in the folder of the one you are in, up to four. Two sit side by side, three are one on the left and two stacked on the right, four are two by two, all the same size. `⌘1` to `⌘4` focus a terminal, `⌘]` and `⌘[` move to the next and the previous, `⇧⌘W` closes the focused one. The focused terminal has a thin outline; typing, find, the command marks and the title bar follow it.
- **Each terminal has a small chip:** a dot while a Claude runs in it (working, or needs you), its folder, and a ✕. With several terminals the ✕ closes that one; on the last one it reads *Restart terminal* and starts a fresh shell in the same folder. If a command or Claude runs there, a question appears under the chip (*A command is running: …* or *Claude is running here*) with *End terminal* and *Cancel*; Cancel is the default and Esc cancels. Nothing running: no question.
- **Each terminal has its own Claude.** The title bar, the companion and the Now card follow the focused terminal's Claude, and a Claude finishing in another terminal still counts: its summary names the folder. A command in any terminal keeps the Mac awake, and `⌘Q` asks when any of them runs a command or Claude.
- **Nothing is saved or replayed.** The layout lives in memory: the next start is one terminal. Quitting still ends every shell.
- **Resume goes into the terminal you are in**, and only at its idle prompt; if it is busy, the command is copied as before.

### Privacy and permissions
- **Where.** *Settings → Privacy and permissions*, and the last step of first run. Five switches, each with a plain sentence about what it reads, sends or does: *Explain the last answer with a helper Claude* (off), *Search the text of past conversations* (off), *Type the resume line for you* (on; off: a click only copies the command), *Move conversations to the Trash* (on; off: no remove buttons) and *Answer permission prompts from the notification* (off; it moved here from *Claude Code*).
- **Always locked, no switch.** *Allow* on a permission prompt needs your own click with the whole request shown. Jaffer never reads Claude's login files or your keychain. Nothing is deleted for good. Everything shown is redacted and a sensitive command is never named.
- **Honest about what a switch is.** Any program of your user account can talk to Jaffer's socket (as it can type into your terminal through it), so a switch records your consent and keeps Jaffer's own screens in line; it is not a lock against your own processes.

### Claude insight
- **Context meter** in the Now card: how full Claude's context is after its last answer (*Context 62% of 1M*), yellow from 80 % and red from 92 %, one toast at 85 % per session. A model the table does not know shows tokens only. It is an estimate.
- **Last 5 hours:** the estimated cost and tokens of Claude's answers in the last five hours across your conversations. It says it is not your plan's limit.
- **Today:** a collapsible list of the last twenty turns (time, length, tool counts, files, cost).
- **Weekly summary:** when Jaffer starts after a week has ended, once, a notification (and a toast with *Open panel*) says what last week cost and how long Claude worked. *Settings → Claude Code → Weekly summary* turns it off. The weekly and last week's numbers are kept in `config.json` (numbers only).
- **Budgets.** *Daily work-time budget* (minutes; 0 is off) colours "Claude worked … today" and says once when it is passed. *Project budgets* (one `~/code/app=25` line per folder, dollars a week) tint that folder's row in *Cost by folder* and toast once a week when passed. They count only the 30 newest conversations, and say so.
- **One switch for all of it:** the context, the last 5 hours and the budgets' money read token counts from your conversation files, so they follow *Show what each answer cost* (its hint now says so). With it off, none of them is read.

### Past conversations
- **Tags:** up to three per conversation (a tag menu on each row, 20 characters each), shown as coloured chips; click a tag chip to filter. **Hide:** an eye button per row; hidden ones go away and *Hidden (N)* shows them dimmed, with *Unhide*. Both are your own words and ids in `config.json`.
- **Storage:** a *Storage* toggle shows how much space your conversations use and the ten biggest, each with a trash button.
- **Clear old:** *Remove…* has *Older than 30 / 60 / 90 days…*, up to 200 at a time. Same question first, same Trash, same refusal of a running conversation.
- **Search text** (needs its switch): the panel's search can look inside conversations. It scans the 200 newest, at most 2 MB of each, for words you and Claude wrote (not tool inputs and outputs, command output or Claude's thinking), shows only which conversations match and how many times, never a snippet, and keeps nothing.

### Setup list
A third section of the side panel, collapsed at first. For the focused terminal's folder it lists MCP servers (from `.mcp.json`), skills, rules and memory files (`CLAUDE.md`, `.claude/rules`), plugins, subagents and hooks (event names and counts, never commands). It only reads files; it changes nothing and never opens `~/.claude.json`. *Check MCP servers* is the one thing that runs something, and only on your click: it runs `claude mcp list` in that folder, which starts your servers briefly, for at most 25 seconds. You see a state for each (connected, needs authentication, failed, pending approval, disabled), never an argument or a URL.

### Explain (opt-in)
With *Explain the last answer with a helper Claude* on, a button in the Now card (after a turn) reads Claude's latest reply, redacts it, keeps the last 6000 characters and sends it to a separate `claude -p` run: no tools, no MCP servers, no saved session, an empty temporary folder, on your own Claude subscription (it uses your plan). The answer is shown as plain text in a card you can dismiss. Without the switch Jaffer never reads a reply. *Ask Claude to explain* is always there and only copies a prompt for you to paste.

### Learn
- *Help → Glossary…* (and the palette) explains twenty words in plain language, with a search. Small ⓘ buttons next to the Now card, the cost, the context meter, the turn bars, the Setup groups, past conversations and a few switches open a short explanation and a link to the entry.
- *Help → Take the Tour* is a one-minute tour with coach marks over the real window (terminal, Now card, past conversations, the palette, split terminals, Setup, Privacy); it is offered once after first run and writes only that you finished it.
- **Tips:** the first time compaction, a helper Claude, a permission prompt, a full context, a turn over five minutes or a passed budget comes up, a quiet note asks if you want to know what it is. At most one in 90 seconds; *Turn tips off* or *Settings → Help* switches them off.

### Limits, honestly
- Four terminals in the smallest window are cramped (about 18 columns each); the title bar's port chip and the find bar follow the focused terminal only.
- The context window table is checked against Anthropic's documentation of 2026-10-16 and has to be updated from it; Claude Code's transcript format is internal and can change.
- A project budget for a folder outside the five busiest has no row to tint; its toast still comes.
- The weekly summary is made at app start only. An older week is not told afterwards.
- Explain runs `claude -p --no-session-persistence --safe-mode …`; that no transcript is written for it has not been run against a real Claude. If your Claude Code is too old for the flags, the button is not offered.
- `claude mcp list` has no machine-readable output, so Jaffer reads its text by the documented status words; anything else shows as unknown.
- The README's *Not verified here* lists everything that has been checked only in a browser and with stand-ins.

**What happens when you update**
- The update itself moves and deletes nothing. New settings appear with their defaults: `permissions` (`explain` off, `deepSearch` off, `resumeTyping` on, `trash` on), `tour`, `tips` (on), `history.tags` and `history.hidden` (empty), `claude.dailyWorkBudgetMin` (0), `claude.projectBudgets` (none), `notifications.weeklyDigest` (on) and the `usage` numbers. `claude.approvals` keeps its value. Jaffer's hooks in `~/.claude/settings.json` stay as they were.

Updating from 0.2.x or later asks first, as always. 0.1.x apps have no updater: install this version from this page once.
