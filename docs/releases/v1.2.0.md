## What is new in 1.2.0

**Jaffer now shows what changed after an update, and adds fourteen small things.**
- **Past conversations:** rename, pin, folder chips, cost by folder, resume the last one with `⇧⌘R`, find them in the palette.
- **Now card:** turn bars and *Claude worked 1h 12m today*.
- **Terminal:** copy a block as an image, recent commands by folder, `⌘/` for a shortcuts sheet, drop-down mode on the hotkey.
- **A seasonal mole** around Halloween, the winter holidays and Valentine's Day.
- **Permission prompts from the notification:** experimental, off by default (*Settings → Claude Code*).
- **This card:** *Help → What's New…* opens it again any time.

**Jaffer now tells you what changed after an update, and adds fourteen small things around the terminal, the past conversations and the Now card.** It is still one window, one shell and a companion with one side panel: no tabs, no splits, no second window, no chat box. Nothing here saves your terminal, and nothing is typed into your shell except `claude` and, after your own action, the resume line of a past conversation.

### What's New
- **After an update, one card at the top of the side panel** shows what is new in the versions you have not seen (the five newest at most). A short message, *Jaffer updated to 1.2.0*, has a *What's new* button that opens the panel with the card. *Dismiss* hides it for good; *All versions* lists every version's notes, the newest open. The card also opens from *Help → What's New…*, from the command palette, and from the menu bar icon's menu (a *What's New in 1.2.0* line that is there only until you dismiss the card).
- **The notes are part of the app.** Nothing is fetched from the network, and the only thing Jaffer remembers is the newest version you dismissed (`updates.seenVersion` in `config.json`). A first start shows nothing.

### Past conversations
- **Rename** a conversation with the pencil on its row, or press `F2` on the selected row. Enter saves, Esc cancels, an empty name removes yours. Your name wins over the title everywhere (the rows, the search, the palette and the menu bar's list). It is your own label, stored in `config.json` (at most 200 names of 80 characters); a title Claude Code wrote is never copied into it.
- **Pin** a conversation with the star: pinned ones sit in a *Pinned* group above the days (up to 50), and stay listed even when they are older than the 50 newest.
- **Folder chips** above the list (*All* and the five folders you used most) show one folder's conversations. They need two folders or more.
- **Cost by folder** (under the chips, only with *Show what each answer cost*): today's and this week's estimate for the five busiest folders. It says what it counts: the 30 newest conversations, at API list prices, an estimate and not a bill.
- **Resume last conversation** (`⇧⌘R`, also in the palette and the *Shell* menu): resumes the newest conversation that began in your shell's folder, else the newest of all. It always reads the list first, so it never resumes a stale one. It follows the rules of a click: it types only at an idle prompt, and while anything runs it copies the command and says why.
- **The command palette finds conversations.** Type two letters or more and up to five matching conversations (by your name, the title or the folder) appear under *Conversation*; Enter resumes the one you picked, like a click.
- **What this changes about typing.** The resume line was typed only after a click on a row. Now `⇧⌘R` and Enter on a conversation in the palette type it too, through the same code and the same checks. Nothing is typed without your keypress or click, and nothing else is typed. Jaffer reads the list of conversations now also when the palette opens or `⇧⌘R` runs with the panel closed, with the same bounds as before: only the id, the folder, the times, the title and the estimated cost, never anything else you or Claude said.

### The Now card
- **Turn bars.** Once Claude has finished two turns, the card draws the last twenty as small bars: the height is how long a turn took, the tint how much it cost compared with the others (equal costs look alike). Hover a bar for the exact time and cost.
- **Claude worked 1h 12m today.** The time of today's finished turns plus the one running now. It starts again at midnight and when Jaffer quits; a turn you cut short with Esc is not counted, so the figure can be lower than the clock. It is not an estimate of money and does not depend on the cost switches.
- **A seasonal companion.** Around Halloween (Oct 24 to Nov 1), the winter holidays (Dec 15 to Jan 3) and Valentine's Day (Feb 12 to 15) the mole wears a small pumpkin, a Santa hat or a heart, and the other scenes get a few drifting dots. It stops with *Animations* off or macOS *Reduce motion*, and *Settings → Appearance → Seasonal touches* switches it off.

### The terminal
- **Copy a block as an image.** Right-click the mark beside a command and choose *Copy as image*: the command and its output (up to 200 lines of 160 columns, the rest cut with "…") are drawn in your terminal's font and colours and put on the clipboard as a PNG. Nothing is saved. A sensitive command is not drawn at all, only its output.
- **Recent commands by folder.** `⌘R` has a *By folder* switch that groups the list by the folder each command ran in, the newest folder first. A pick still only copies.
- **A shortcuts sheet.** `⌘/` (or *Help → Keyboard Shortcuts*, or the palette) lists every shortcut by section, with a search. Esc closes it.
- **Drop-down mode.** *Settings → Appearance → Window → Drop-down mode* (it needs the hotkey): the hotkey brings Jaffer down from the top of the screen under the pointer, the full width and about 45 % of the height, and it hides when you click elsewhere. Your normal window size and place are left alone and come back when you switch it off. A window in full screen is not moved.

### Permission prompts from the notification (experimental, off by default)
- **What it is.** *Settings → Claude Code → Answer permission prompts from the notification.* When Claude Code asks to use a tool, Jaffer shows a notification, *Claude asks to use Bash*, with one redacted line of what it asks (at most 120 characters) and the buttons **Show** and **Deny**: never Allow. The title bar and the Now card show the same question with a countdown, and Deny.
- **Allow is hard to press on purpose.** It exists only in the Now card, next to the whole line, only when the request is shown whole (a command that was cut, that contained a secret Jaffer masked, a sensitive command or file, or a tool with no line to show gets only *Deny* and *Answer in terminal*), only 0.75 seconds after it appeared, and only to a real mouse click: not the keyboard, not a script. Edits and writes of files (Edit, Write, MultiEdit, NotebookEdit) are never answered with Allow from Jaffer, because what they would write is not shown: *Deny* or *Answer in terminal*. Deny is always safe.
- **Silence is Claude Code's own prompt.** Show, *Answer in terminal*, no click within 20 seconds, the setting turned off, Jaffer closed or any error: Jaffer says nothing and Claude Code asks you as it always did. With the setting on, Jaffer adds a twelfth hook (`PermissionRequest`) to `~/.claude/settings.json`, the only one that makes Claude Code wait, for at most 20 seconds; turned off, it takes it out again.
- **What it needs.** The window must be out of the front for a notification (when it is in front, the title bar and the panel ask), and macOS must allow Jaffer's notifications with their buttons (some styles show them only under *Options*). Like any notification, its text stays in macOS's own notification list.
- **Not checked against every Claude Code.** That Claude Code's permission hook waits for Jaffer before it draws its own dialog, and that silence brings its own prompt back, is taken from its documentation and has not been run against a real session. This is why it is off, and why it says *Experimental*.

### Already there
These ideas from the plan were already in Jaffer, so 1.2.0 does not change them: the **recap after a turn** (a message with how long, what it did, the files and the cost), the **files Claude edited** in the Now card, **copy as Markdown** (`⌥⌘C`, and in the mark's menu), **following macOS light and dark** (*Settings → Appearance*) and the **translucent window** (*Blur* and the opacity slider).

### Limits, honestly
- The menu bar item, the Help entries, the drop-down mode with several displays, Spaces and a full-screen app, and the notification buttons and their order have not been used on a real Mac yet: the README's *Not verified here* lists them.
- Turn bars, the worked time and the seasonal looks were checked in the dark themes only.
- `⌘/` goes through the macOS menu; on a keyboard where `/` needs Shift it may differ.
- The title of a conversation still depends on how your version of Claude Code writes its transcripts.

**What happens when you update**
- Nothing is moved or deleted. New settings appear with their defaults: `updates.seenVersion`, `history.names`, `history.pinned`, `claude.approvals` (off), `window.dropdown` (off) and `appearance.seasonal` (on). Jaffer's hooks in `~/.claude/settings.json` stay the same eleven until you switch on the permission prompts. Updating from 0.5.x or earlier still runs the one-time clean-up of 0.6.0 (see below).

**Coming from 0.5 or earlier?** Jaffer has been a plain terminal since 0.6.0: the memory, the saved screen, *Resume Claude* and *Restart Claude Code*, the login agent and the Safety edges are gone, and quitting ends the shell and anything running in it (`⌘Q` asks first when Claude Code is working or a command runs; a logout, restart or shutdown and the Dock's *Quit* do not ask). On the first start Jaffer cleans up what they left, once (up to 30 seconds): it removes the login agent, **copies `~/.jaffer/memory/NOTES.md` and `POLICY.md` to `~/.jaffer-memory-backup/`** if you wrote something in them, **deletes `~/.jaffer/memory` and `~/.jaffer/session`**, removes only Jaffer's own entries from Claude Code's files (the marked block in `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md`, the `jaffer-*` skills, the `jaffer` MCP server) and drops the settings that no longer exist. **To keep anything from the memory, copy `~/.jaffer/memory` before you open this version for the first time** (if you update from inside the app, copy it before you click *Update and restart*). `jaffer config set migrated 0` runs the clean-up again; *Settings → Reset* does the same and more. If an older Jaffer's session is still running in the background, one dialog asks to end it (the default button is Quit).

Updating from 0.2.x or later asks first, as always. 0.1.x apps have no updater: install this version from this page once.
